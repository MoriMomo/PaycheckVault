What to Learn and Apply
From YieldGuard (closest competitor on Reactive Network)

Study how they present the "why Reactive?" argument in their README and demo. Since judges will compare you directly, your README needs one clear paragraph that says: "YieldGuard reacts to pool rate events. PaycheckVault reacts to inbound transfers to a specific recipient — an event the vault itself cannot observe without an external watcher. That's the same architecture, different trigger, different user story."

Your contracts already do this correctly. The gap is just making sure the README says it explicitly.

From Reccura (recurring payments without keepers)

Their key insight: frame the absence of a keeper as a feature, not just an implementation detail. In your demo video and README, make a point of saying "no Gelato subscription, no Chainlink Automation fee, no backend server." Reccura leads with this and it lands well with judges who understand the trust/cost tradeoffs.

From YO Treasury (UI)

Their allocation UI uses visual split bars and preset buttons — which your dashboard already has. The one thing worth borrowing: their labeling. Instead of calling it "50% swap," they frame it as "Accumulation Strategy" vs "Liquid Reserve." That framing makes it sound like a wealth management tool, not a script. Your dashboard already uses "Auto-DCA" and "Liquid Savings" — keep those, they're already on the right track.

From DeFi AI Agent Consultant (user story)

They described your exact product but never built it. The lesson: own the story they wrote. In your DoraHacks submission description, you can directly say: "This is the working implementation of the recipient-side auto-allocation vault that existing payroll protocols don't provide."
